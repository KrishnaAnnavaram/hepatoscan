---
title: CT scans of the liver
source: General patient-education summary written for this project; not clinically reviewed
---
A CT (computed tomography) scan uses X-rays to make many thin cross-section images of the body. Together the images form a 3-D volume.

Doctors often give a contrast medium into a vein before an abdominal CT. The contrast makes blood vessels and some liver lesions easier to see. The appearance of a lesion can change between the contrast phases.

CT images use Hounsfield units, a scale of tissue density. Normal liver tissue usually looks brighter than many lesions on a contrast scan, but this is not always true.
